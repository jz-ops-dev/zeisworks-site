Google changed the rules of search again — and if you run a small business website, these changes hit different than they do for big brands with dedicated SEO teams.

This isn't a summary of SEO blog posts about the updates. This is a practical breakdown of what actually changed in Google's systems between late 2025 and mid-2026, what the data shows about who's winning and losing, and the concrete steps small business owners should take right now.

## What Actually Happened: The 2025–2026 Timeline

### The AI Overviews Rollout (Ongoing from 2024 through 2026)

Google's AI Overviews — the generated summaries that appear at the top of search results — launched broadly in 2024 and expanded to over 100 countries through 2025. By early 2026, they appear on a significant share of informational and research queries in Google's biggest markets (US, India, UK, and growing).

The impact on click-through rates was severe at launch. Ahrefs measured a 58% drop in clicks for the number-one ranking page when an AI Overview is present on informational queries. Seer Interactive found a 61% fall in organic CTR on those same query types. The user got the answer in the overview and never clicked through.

But the data shifted in early 2026. After 18 months of tracking CTR decline, Seer Interactive reported the trend reversed: organic CTR on AI Overview queries climbed from a floor of 1.3% in December 2025 to 2.4% in February 2026. Google may be adjusting the layout, source placement, or the types of queries that trigger an overview. The worst of the traffic bleed may be over for sites that remain visible.

**Where AI Overviews appear:**
- Strong informational intent: "how does X work," "what should I do when Y happens"
- Research and comparison queries: "what is the difference between A and B"
- Growing presence on local queries, though the local pack still dominates for "near me" searches

**Where they don't (consistently) appear:**
- Navigational queries ("plumber in Denver")
- Highly transactional queries ("book appointment now")
- Branded searches

### The Search Console Impressions Bug (May 2025 – April 2026)

This one flew under the radar for most small business owners, but it matters for interpreting your data.

Google confirmed a bug that inflated Search Console impression counts for approximately 50 weeks — from May 13, 2025 through April 27, 2026. A separate logging error also prevented about 50 weeks of data from being recorded at all.

**What this means for you:** Your pre-April 2026 impression data is unreliable for trend analysis. If you've been watching impressions climb since last summer, some of that growth may have been the bug, not real performance gains.

Click data was not affected. When analyzing your site's performance around the March core update, use clicks as your primary metric, not impressions. Compare February 27–March 26 (your pre-update baseline) against April 9–May 6 (post-update) for a clean comparison window.

### The February 2026 Discover Core Update (February 5–27)

Google released its first-ever Discover-only core update — an unprecedented move. The update took 21 days to roll out and targeted three things:

1. **More locally relevant content** in Discover feeds
2. **Reduced clickbait** — sensational headlines that don't deliver
3. **Original and in-depth reporting** over rehashed material

The impact was measurable: the number of unique domains appearing in US Discover feeds dropped from 172 to 158. Google tightened the pool of sites it considers authoritative enough to feature.

This update applied to English-language content in the US only, with plans to expand to other languages and regions later in 2026. If you rely on Discover traffic and publish outside the US, expect similar changes when your market is included.

### The March 2026 Spam Update (March 24–25)

Completed in less than 20 hours — the shortest confirmed spam update on record. It targeted cloaking, manipulative link schemes, and thin or AI-generated content published at scale purely to game rankings.

This update is important context for what came next. Google cleared spam signals first, then launched the broad core update two days later. Sites propped up by manipulative link-building lost their supports before the core update arrived to judge content quality in a cleaner environment. Some sites were caught by both.

### The March 2026 Core Update (March 27 – April 8)

The first broad core update of 2026 — and by almost every measure, it was more disruptive than the December 2025 update that preceded it.

**The headline numbers:**
- SEMrush Sensor volatility peaked at 9.5 out of 10
- More than 55% of monitored websites experienced measurable ranking shifts in the first two weeks
- 79.5% of top-three results changed position during the rollout (up from 66.8% in December)
- About 24% of pages ranking in the top 10 dropped out of the top 100 entirely
- Sites on the wrong side reported organic traffic drops of 20–35%

This was the third algorithm change in five weeks. The sequence — Discover update, then spam update, then core update — was not coincidence. Google clean up the environment before reassessing quality.

**What the March core update actually targeted:**

**1. E-E-A-T got significantly more weight.** Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness signals were tightened more than in any prior core update. Industry data shows 72% of top-ranking pages now display detailed author credentials, up from around 58% before this update. The "Experience" component in particular — first-hand, real-world experience with the subject matter — was a clear differentiator.

**2. Information Gain scoring increased.** Google increased weighting on how much genuinely new information your content adds compared to what already ranks. Pages that simply reword existing top results without contributing fresh data, original perspectives, or unique analysis lost ground. This aligns with a concept Google has patented and discussed in research papers for years, but the March update appears to be the first time it was meaningfully deployed at scale.

**3. Sharper AI content detection.** This is widely believed to be the first core update using Google's Gemini 4.0 Semantic Filter. Importantly, this does not penalize AI-assisted content outright. It targets pages where AI was used to produce content at scale without meaningful editorial oversight. The pattern that gets demoted is mass-produced content lacking original insight — regardless of the writing tool.

**4. Site-wide Core Web Vitals assessment.** Technical performance is now evaluated more holistically across your entire site rather than page by page. Sites with inconsistent performance — fast homepage but slow blog posts — are being penalized. Notably, 43% of websites still fail the 200ms INP (Interaction to Next Paint) threshold.

**5. Topical authority deepening.** Google is evaluating your entire site's depth in specific subject areas, not just individual pages. A plumbing company with 200 pages of genuinely useful plumbing content has an advantage over a general home services site with 20 plumbing pages mixed in with electrical, HVAC, and landscaping.

### Google I/O 2026: AI Agents and the New Search Box

At Google I/O on May 19, 2026, Google announced what it called the biggest upgrade to the search box in over 25 years. The key announcements:

**AI Agents in Search:** Users can now trigger AI agents directly from search to complete tasks — not just find information, but book appointments, compare options, and take actions. For service businesses, this means the path from "find a plumber" to "book a plumber" is shrinking.

**Four new ad formats inside AI Overviews:** Google expanded ads in AI Overviews to desktop in the US, with international expansion planned. Ads also began appearing inside AI Mode (Google's most powerful AI search experience). Businesses already using Performance Max, Shopping, and Search campaigns with broad match will be eligible.

**New "Perspectives" carousel and "highly cited" labels:** AI Overviews now show more diverse source types and highlight which sources are most frequently cited across similar queries. This creates a new visibility opportunity: being a frequently-cited source in your niche.

The practical impact for small businesses: AI Overviews and AI Mode are now monetized surfaces. The businesses most likely to be cited and to have their ads shown in these new formats are those with strong topical authority, clear author credentials, and content structured to be extracted as a direct answer.

## Who Lost, Who Won, and Why

Based on analysis across the SEO community, industry tools, and early ranking data:

### Patterns that lost visibility:
- Mass-produced AI content without human editorial oversight
- Thin affiliate pages aggregating product info without genuine testing
- Coupon and deal aggregator sites with no editorial value
- Clickbait headlines that don't deliver on their promise
- Pages with weak or absent author attribution
- Sites with inconsistent technical performance across pages

### Patterns that gained visibility:
- Original, experience-driven content with genuine first-hand insights
- Pages with strong E-E-A-T signals — author bylines, credentials, transparent attribution
- Sites demonstrating deep topical authority in specific verticals
- Content delivering clear information gain over existing page-one results
- Businesses with verified, complete Google Business Profiles

## What Small Businesses Should Do Now

### 1. Audit Your Authors and Editorial Trust

Every piece of content on your site should make it clear who is responsible for the advice and why they're qualified to give it. Use a real author or editorial team. Keep bylines consistent. Make author bios useful — include credentials, years of experience, and a photo.

This is not about adding fake expertise. If you're a plumber with 15 years of experience, say so. If your office manager writes your blog posts, have them interview you first and attribute the insights.

### 2. Add Original Value or Stop Publishing

Ask of every page: "Could someone get this exact information from ten other generic summaries?" If yes, the page needs to change or come down.

Strong pages include first-party data, a clear methodology, direct experience, official sources, or a defensible point of view. Label estimates as estimates. Explain how you reached your conclusions.

For service businesses, original value looks like: photos from actual job sites (with permission), specific pricing ranges you've observed in your market, step-by-step processes you actually follow, and case studies from your own work.

### 3. Fix Your Google Business Profile (Still the Most Underrated SEO Move)

Local SEO ranking factors for 2026 continue to center on the Google Business Profile. The businesses showing up in the local pack have:

- Fully completed profiles (every field filled, every relevant attribute selected)
- Consistent weekly posts tied to seasonal topics and services
- 10+ real photos updated regularly
- Review response rates above 90%, within 48 hours
- Active Q&A sections where the business owner answers questions

The Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors study confirms: proximity, relevance, and prominence still drive local rankings. Your Business Profile is the single most controllable factor.

### 4. Improve Your Site-Wide Technical Performance

Run your site through Google PageSpeed Insights and WebPageTest. The March update evaluates performance site-wide, not page-by-page. If your homepage loads in 1.2 seconds but your blog posts take 4 seconds, that inconsistency hurts.

Priority fixes:
- Reduce INP below 200ms (43% of sites still fail this)
- Compress and properly size images
- Minimize render-blocking resources
- Use lazy loading for below-fold content

### 5. Optimize for AI Overview Citations

If your content answers questions people ask, structure it to be cited:

- Write clear, direct answers to common questions — the kind that could stand alone as a paragraph in an AI Overview
- Include author attribution and credentials on every article
- Use structured data (FAQ schema, HowTo schema, Article schema)
- Build topical depth — cover a subject comprehensively across multiple pages rather than one thin page per topic

### 6. Stop Panicking, Start Tracking

The worst response to a core update is to overhaul everything overnight. Establish a clean baseline in Search Console using click data (not impressions). Compare your February 27–March 26 performance against April 9–May 6. Segment by:

- Brand queries vs. non-brand queries
- Pages that lost impressions vs. pages that lost CTR
- Informational content vs. transactional/service pages

Track weekly. Don't make changes until you've identified the specific pattern of what moved and why.

## The Bottom Line

Google's 2025–2026 changes are not one update — they're a consistent direction. Every change moves in the same direction: reward genuine expertise, original value, and real-world experience. Penalize mass-produced content, manipulative tactics, and pages that exist only to capture search traffic.

For small business owners, the path is straightforward:

1. **Show your expertise** — make it clear who you are and why you know what you know
2. **Add original value** — share what you've actually seen, done, and learned
3. **Fix the basics** — Google Business Profile, site speed, mobile experience
4. **Be consistent** — one quality article per month beats ten thin ones
5. **Track without panic** — use the right data, over the right time windows

The businesses that do this don't just survive algorithm changes. They gain ground every time Google raises the bar.
